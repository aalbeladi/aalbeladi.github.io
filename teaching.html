Teaching

I want students to leave a course understanding what a model means, when it is useful, and what can go wrong when its assumptions are ignored.

Current courses

  • IE 255: Engineering Economy

    Economic reasoning for engineering decisions, from cash-flow equivalence and the time value of money to comparing alternatives, rate-of-return analysis, replacement decisions, and project evaluation.

    Topics: time value of money, present and annual worth, rate of return, MARR, replacement analysis

  • IE 490: Industrial Engineering Seminar and Cooperative Training

    A bridge between academic industrial engineering and professional practice: how engineers frame ambiguous problems, communicate analysis, and turn technical work into decisions in real organizations.

    Emphasis: problem framing, technical communication, industry exposure, career preparation

Approach

Intuition before notation

Start with the engineering decision and its tradeoff, then introduce the mathematics.

Assumptions made visible

Students learn what a model assumes and how the answer changes when an assumption fails.

Real applications

Examples come from infrastructure, energy, operations, and investment decisions.

Teaching interests

  • Operations Research

    Optimization modeling, duality, linear and mixed-integer programming, and decision analysis.

  • Stochastic Optimization

    Decision making under uncertainty: scenarios, recourse, chance constraints, and decomposition.

  • Energy Systems Optimization

    Planning, reliability, infrastructure investment, policy, and markets through an operations-research lens.
